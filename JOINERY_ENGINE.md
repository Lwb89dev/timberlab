# Joinery

The first set is implemented. The rule is in `docs/adr/0005-joinery.md`.

A joint is an OCCT boolean on the part solids. The viewport mesh is a tessellation of that solid. A decorative mesh, a texture, or a second shape drawn on top of an untouched board is not a joint. The record stores the type, both part ids, the faces, and the sizes. Those sizes are applied again when a part changes, so a centered tenon follows a thickness edit that still fits. An edit that makes a stored joint not fit is rejected and the document stays as it was. The mesh does not fall back to the uncut solid.

Stable identifiers are not localized:

- `joint.mortise_tenon` — cuts both parts. Part A is the mortise, part B is the tenon.
- `joint.half_lap` — cuts both parts. `removeMinA` and `removeMinB` choose the low-U half.
- `joint.dado` — cuts part A across the full V axis of the face. Part B is the stored mate and is not cut.
- `joint.groove` — cuts a stopped slot in part A. Part B is the stored mate and is not cut.
- `joint.rabbet` — cuts one edge of part A's face. Part B is the stored mate and is not cut. US “rabbet”, UK “rebate”; one identifier.
- `joint.bridle` — cuts both parts. Part A keeps a centered slot. Part B keeps a centered tongue and loses both cheeks. The slot is `thickness` wide and `length` deep.
- `joint.castle` — cuts both parts. `count` (2 to 8) is the number of fingers on part A. The bands are equal, centered, and `thickness` wide. Part A loses the odd bands. Part B loses the even bands.
- `joint.miter` — cuts both parts. The cutter is a right-triangle prism, not a box. One leg is `length` along the face normal. The other leg is the full U size. `removeMinA` and `removeMinB` keep the low-U corner. The angle is 45 degrees when `length` equals that U size.
- `joint.dovetail` — through dovetail. Part A is the pins, part B is the tails. `count` (1 to 8) is the number of tails. `thickness` is the tail width at the tail end, `width` is the pin width on that same plane, and `offset` is the slope on each side. `length` reaches into the tails. `depth` reaches into the pins and may equal the pin thickness, which is through.
- `joint.sliding_dovetail` — cuts both parts. Part A loses a trapezoid dado that runs the full V of the face. `width` is the opening at the face, `offset` is its U position, and `offset2` is the slope on each side. Part B loses the two shoulders of a centered tongue.
- `joint.finger` — cuts both parts. `count` (2 to 8) equal bands fill the U axis. Both parts must share that U, and U must divide by `count`. Part A loses the odd bands. Part B loses the even bands. `length` is the engagement. The cut runs the full V. The band width is U / `count` and is not stored.
- `joint.half_blind_dovetail` — the same prisms as `joint.dovetail`. `depth` must stay short of the pin board's normal, so the show face stays solid. A depth that equals that normal is refused. A through dovetail still allows it.
- `joint.tongue_groove` — cuts both parts. Part A loses one centered slot, the full V. Part B loses two shoulders and keeps a centered tongue. `thickness` is that width. `depth` is the slot depth and the tongue length. The tongue must keep at least 2 nm of U outside the tongue, so both shoulders exist.
- `joint.dowel` — cuts both parts. Each face gets one centered cylindrical hole. `thickness` is the diameter and must be an even number of nanometres, so the radius is an integer. `depth` may equal the face normal. The radius stays inside both faces.
- `joint.haunched_tenon` — cuts both parts. A mortise and tenon whose tenon keeps a short haunch beside it. Part A is the mortise, part B is the tenon, with the same `thickness`, `length`, `depth`, `offset`, and `offset2` as `joint.mortise_tenon`. `width` is the tenon along V. The rest of the tenon end's V is the haunch, and `length2` is the haunch length at the shoulder. The tenon cut is the cheeks over the tenon, the tip removed over the haunch, and the cheeks of the haunch. The mortise is the stepped pocket of both: `depth` deep for the tenon, `length2` deep for the haunch. The haunch is on the high-V side of each part. `removeMinB` moves it to the low-V side of the tenon, `removeMinA` to the low-V side of the mortise. `width` must stay under the tenon end's V, `length2` under `length` and at most `depth`, and the mortise needs the whole tenon end's V.
- `joint.wedged_tenon` — cuts both parts. The mortise and tenon of `joint.mortise_tenon`, plus `count` (1 or 2) saw kerfs in the tenon tip. `width2` is the kerf width along V and `length2` is the kerf depth from the tip. A kerf runs across the tenon thickness. One kerf sits at the middle of the tenon width, two sit at a quarter and three quarters. `length2` stays within `length`, and the kerfs stay inside the tenon width. The wedge itself is not a part. The mortise is not flared.
- `joint.loose_tenon` — cuts both parts. Each face gets `count` (1 to 8) pockets for a separate tenon, centered on U. `thickness` is the pocket on U, `width` is its length on V, and `depth` is its depth in each part, so the loose tenon is twice that long. `offset` is the pitch between pockets when `count` is above 1 and must be at least `width`. The row is centered on V. The loose tenon is not a part.
- `joint.cross_lap` — cuts both parts. Part A loses a full-V slot from `faceA`, part B loses one from `faceB`. `depth` is what part A loses. Part B loses what is left of its own normal, `normal − depth`, so the two remainders add up to one board. Each slot is as wide as the other part's V extent: part A's slot is part B's V wide, and part B's slot is part A's V wide. `offset` places A's slot on U and `offset2` places B's. The slots follow a change of the other part's V.
- `joint.end_lap` — cuts both parts. The end of part B laps into a notch across part A's face. This is a tee half lap. It is not the corner lap, which is `joint.half_lap`. Part A loses a full-V notch on `faceA`, `width` wide on U at `offset` and `depth` deep. Part B's end on `faceB` keeps a tongue `depth` thick over `length` and loses the rest of its U. `removeMinB` keeps the high-U side instead of the low one. `depth` must stay under both A's normal and B's U.
- `joint.splined_miter` — cuts both parts. The miter of `joint.miter`, plus a kerf across each mitered face for a spline. `thickness` is the kerf along V, `offset` is where it starts on V in both parts, and `depth` is how far it reaches into the material, perpendicular to the miter face. The kerf covers the whole miter face and opens at the outer corner, so the spline shows there. It is a four-point prism. `depth` must keep the kerf inside the board's length.
- `joint.biscuit` — cuts both parts. Each face gets `count` (1 to 8) disc-shaped kerfs, centered on U. `thickness` is the kerf, `width` is the disc diameter and must be even, and `depth` is the plunge into each part and at most the radius. The kerf is the cap of a cylinder whose axis runs along U, so its chord on the face is `2·sqrt(depth·(diameter − depth))`. `offset` is the pitch between kerfs when `count` is above 1 and must be at least that chord. The biscuit is not a part.
- `joint.pocket_screw` — cuts part A only. Part B is the stored mate and is not cut. `faceA` is the end face of A that meets B, and `edge` (`u0`, `u1`, `v0`, `v1`) is the side of that face where the hole enters. The hole runs from that side toward the end at 15 degrees and exits on the end face. It is a counterbore of diameter `width` and length `length` along the hole, then a pilot of diameter `thickness` out to the end face. Both diameters must be even and `thickness` smaller. `offset` is the distance from the end face to the entry point. `count` (1 to 8) holes sit `offset2` apart along the other axis and must be at least `width` apart. The counterbore opening and the pilot exit must stay inside the part. The cutter is an axis cylinder at 15 degrees, the first one that is not parallel to a part axis.

Faces are `x0`, `x1`, `y0`, `y1`, `z0`, `z1` in the part's local frame. `x0` is the minimum X face. On a face, U and V are the other two axes: X faces use U = Y and V = Z, Y faces use U = X and V = Z, Z faces use U = X and V = Y. A rabbet edge is `u0`, `u1`, `v0`, or `v1`.

Lengths in the joint record are nanometres: `length`, `width`, `thickness`, `depth`, `offset`, `offset2`, `length2`, `width2`. `length2` and `width2` are omitted from the file when they are zero. `count` is an integer, not a length. Castle, through dovetail, finger, half-blind dovetail, wedged tenon, loose tenon, biscuit, and pocket screw require it. It is omitted from the file when it is zero. The editor sends `count` as that integer. It does not parse it as a length. The editor's Add joint fills them with `defaultJoint`. Those starting sizes are not a structural rating, and TimberLab does not present them as one. The joint does not move either part; placement stays where the editor put it. A project parameter can move a part. That edit does not rewrite the joint record. The cut is rebuilt from the stored joint sizes and the new part extents when the joint still fits. An edit that makes the joint not fit is rejected (`joint.parameter`) and the document is unchanged. Loading a file whose joint does not fit the propagated sizes fails the same way.

A box cutter that already lies on a part face is extended 0.2 mm past that face so the boolean is not asked to cut a coincident face. The extra tool volume is outside the solid. A miter, a through dovetail, a half-blind dovetail, and a sliding dovetail are prisms. Their sloped edges are not moved. The extrusion is extended past the part, and a 0.2 mm slab is fused on each profile edge that already lies on a part face. That slab stays outside the part. Interior faces of a pocket are not extended. A dowel is a cylinder. Its radius is the stored radius. Opening the tool past a face does not change that radius. Several cutters on one part are fused, then subtracted once. A boolean that does not run, or that does not reduce the volume, is an error (`joint.no_effect` or `geometry.kernel`). A cylindrical part cannot take a joint (`joint.not_box`).

No joint here is a structural rating. Neither is there a solved assembly that closes the gap between the two parts. The recipes do not cut joints.
